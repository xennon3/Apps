# Super Store Night

A first-person horror prototype set in a 24-hour big-box store. Walk in from the parking lot, find Aisle 14 (Hardware), survive the blackout, and pick a weapon off the pegboard. Then the store locks down. To get out you need your car keys (a zombie has them; listen for the jingle), the manager's key card from the back office, and power to the front doors from the breaker panel. Once you have all three, the Night Manager comes for you, hunting by your light, and you have to make it to the doors. Don't look back when you leave. You will anyway.

Serve the folder (for example `python3 -m http.server`) and open `index.html`. No build step. Three.js r128 loads from cdnjs, and its GLTFLoader, SkeletonUtils and post-processing passes from jsdelivr; all sound is synthesized with the Web Audio API: HRTF 3D panning, a convolution reverb shaped like a big store, and formant-filtered voices for the screams, groans and growls. Headphones recommended.

- `index.html` is the full page.
- Graphics: bloom on the tubes and signs, a film pass (lens fringing, colour grade, vignette, grain), flashlight shadows, glossy scuffed tile, block walls, open joists and ductwork, light shafts under each fixture, drifting dust, price-tag strips and 64 made-up product labels on boxes and cans. Phones get smaller shadow maps and no multisampling.
- `game.html` is the same page without the document wrapper, used when publishing it as a Claude artifact.
- `sounds/monster_roar.wav` is Luther's monster roar (Mixkit, trimmed to 3.2 s). It is the Night Manager's voice: pitched up for the shriek when it spots you, played in full for the final scream at the doors, and slowed and muffled for its distant growls. Everything else is still synthesized.
- The store is watching. The intercom chimes and then speaks, in the browser's own voice where there is one. It knows your aisle, your weapon, your flashlight, whether you're bleeding and whether you've stopped moving. The back office has four live security monitors. Stare at them long enough and something comes to stand behind you, but only on camera. Later it waits at the end of aisle 14, but only on camera. Once the Night Manager is out, CAM 04 tracks it.
- The store remembers. Receipts on the floor come from other nights going back to 1966, all with the same screws, the same initials and a handwritten warning. The staff board by the office lists you on tonight's shift, under your initials. Your initials, visit count and last run are kept in this browser, so the intercom welcomes you back and one receipt is from your last night.
- The store changes. Aisle signs lie while your back is turned and an aisle 0 appears over the centre walkway. The mannequins on the riser by the front only move while nobody is looking. The store radio skips like a stuck disc as things get worse, and lights die around the Night Manager as it moves.
- Weapons are built in code from lathes, tubes and extrusions with worn, blood-spattered metal, wood and rubber that catch the light: a claw hammer, a hex crowbar, a fire axe, a barbed-wire bat with nails, a chainsaw whose chain runs round the bar, and a pump shotgun. The shotgun hangs in a smashed gun case in Sporting Goods (aisle 7). It fires nine pellets, pumps itself, ejects shells that stay on the floor, and wakes every zombie nearby; the Night Manager hears it from across the store. Boxes of buckshot sit in the case, in Garden and under the manager's desk. When it's empty you swing the stock.
- `models/` holds the zombies, fleeing shoppers and props as glTF files (saved with a `.json` extension), trimmed from Quaternius' Zombie Apocalypse Kit (CC0). At load the zombies are repainted into grey, bruised, blood-soaked corpses, given pinprick eyes and stretched taller, and their walk, run, crawl, attack, flinch and death animations are driven by the game logic with a stuttering clock. Two of the zombies are Luther's: the blocky rigged zombie from his asset pack (`zombie_luther.json`, arms held out in code), and his zombie photo, cut into six hinged pieces (`zombie_photo.webp`) and animated as a paper puppet that always turns to face you. The photo zombie carries your car keys. If the models can't load (for example when the page is opened straight from disk), the game falls back to its hand-built figures.

Controls: WASD to move, Shift to run, mouse to look, click to swing or fire (hold with the chainsaw), E to take a weapon, F to toggle the flashlight. Phones get an on-screen stick and buttons.
